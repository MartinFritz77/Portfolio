Predicting Car Sales

EDA and Modeling of bad purchases of US used car dealers with real data and unbalanced target categories

(from my final project in my data science training)

The situation and the goal: A U.S.-based used car dealership purchases used vehicles at low prices through online auctions and from other car dealers in order to resell them profitably on its own platform. Determining whether the purchase of a used vehicle is worthwhile is often challenging. One of the major risks in used car auctions is acquiring vehicles with severe defects that make them unsuitable for resale to customers.

These vehicles are commonly referred to as “lemons” — cars with significant manufacturing defects that substantially impair their safety, usability, or value and cannot be adequately repaired within a reasonable number of repair attempts or within a specified period of time. In such cases, customers are legally entitled to a refund of the purchase price. In addition to the initial acquisition costs, purchasing such “lemons” can therefore result in considerable follow-up expenses, including storage and repair costs, which may ultimately lead to financial losses when reselling the vehicle.

To reduce the number of erroneous purchases involving “lemons,” a predictive model is to be developed that estimates whether a vehicle offered for purchase is likely to turn out to be a “lemon.” At the same time, the model should avoid excluding too many potentially profitable purchases.

Each row in the dataset represents a car that was first purchased at auction and then resold. The data dictionary is as follows:

Column no. column name type of data description
1 'IsBadBuy' categorical (nominal) target variable, (0= used car is not a “lemon”, 1= used car is a “lemon”)
2 'PurchDate' continuous (datetime) The date on which the vehicle was purchased at auction
3 'Auction' categorical (nominal) The auction house where the vehicle was purchased
4 'VehYear' continuous (int) Year of manufacture
5 'VehicleAge' continuous (int) Age of the car at the time of the auction
6 'Make' categorical (nominal) Vehicle manufacturer
7 'Model' categorical (nominal) Vehicle model
8 'Trim' categorical (nominal) Vehicle equipment
9 'SubModel' categorical (nominal) Vehicle submodel
10 'Color' categorical (nominal) Vehicle color
11 'Transmission' categorical (nominal) Vehicle transmission type (automatic, manual)
12 'WheelTypeID' categorical (nominal) The rim type ID
13 'WheelType' categorical (nominal) Type of rims
14 'VehOdo' continuous (int) Vehicle mileage
15 'Nationality' categorical (nominal) The manufacturer’s country
16 'Size' categorical (nominal) The vehicle’s size class (compact, SUV, etc.)
17 'TopThreeAmericanName' categorical (nominal) Determines whether the manufacturer is one of the three leading American manufacturers
18 'MMRAcquisitionAuctionAveragePrice' continuous (int) Purchase price in U.S. dollars for this vehicle in average condition at the time of purchase
19 'MMRAcquisitionAuctionCleanPrice' continuous (int) Purchase price in U.S. dollars for this vehicle, which was in above-average condition at the time of purchase
20 'MMRAcquisitionRetailAveragePrice' continuous (int) Retail purchase price in U.S. dollars for this vehicle in average condition at the time of purchase
21 'MMRAcquisitonRetailCleanPrice' continuous (int) Retail purchase price in U.S. dollars for this vehicle in above-average condition at the time of purchase

24 'MMRCurrentRetailAveragePrice' continuous (int) Retail purchase price in U.S. dollars for this vehicle in average condition as of today
25 'MMRCurrentRetailCleanPrice' continuous (int) Retail purchase price in U.S. dollars for this vehicle in above-average condition as of today
26 'PRIMEUNIT' categorical (nominal) Determines whether the vehicle would generate higher demand than a standard purchase
27 'AUCGUART' categorical (nominal) Warranty level assigned to the vehicle by the auction platform (‘GREEN’ - warranty available, ‘YELLOW’ - warranty status unclear, ‘RED’ - no warranty)
28 'BYRNO' categorical (nominal) A unique number assigned to the buyer who purchased the vehicle
29 'VNZIP1' categorical (nominal) Zip code of the location where the car was purchased
30 'VNST' categorical (nominal) State where the car was purchased
31 'VehBCost' continuous (int) Purchase price in U.S. dollars paid for the vehicle at the time of purchase
32 'IsOnlineSale' categorical (nominal) Determines whether the vehicle was originally purchased online
33 'WarrantyCost' continuous (int) Cost of the warranty for a 36-month term

Preparation

As the target variable is binary and we want to predict a category, classification models will be deployed. The following will be tested: * LogisticRegression * Support Vector Machine/Classifier (SVC) * Random Forest * Artificial Neural Network (ANN)

The following metrics are relevant: * minimize purchase of “lemons”: high recall (actual “lemons” are predcited correctly) * maximize sales of used cars: high precision (predicted “lemons” are actually “lemons”) –> f1 score as the weighted harmonic mean of precision and recall

def clean_data(df, median=None, mode=None, mad=None, outlier_col=None):
    '''
    Function to select columns, transform data types, deal with missings and outliers
    input: features_train or features_test, median, mode, mad from train data if applied on test data;
            outlier_col: columns for which outliers shall be trimmed to 3 * mad
    output: clean features_train, ready for model training + median, mode, mad for usage on test data; 
            clean features_test
    '''
    
    df = df.copy() # to prevent warnings
    
    from scipy.stats import median_abs_deviation
    
    # calculate median, mode and mad from train data
    if median is None:
        median = df.median(numeric_only=True)
    if mode is None:
        mode = df.mode().iloc[0]
    if mad is None:
        mad = df.select_dtypes(include='number').apply(median_abs_deviation)
    
    # format date time column
    df['PurchDate'] = pd.to_datetime(df['PurchDate'], unit='s')
    
    # remove unnecessary columns
    df = df.drop(columns=['Model', 'SubModel', 'Trim', 'Transmission', 'PRIMEUNIT', 'AUCGUART', 'WheelTypeID', 'BYRNO'])
    
    # change data type object in category
    feature_obj = ['Auction', 'Make', 'Color', 'WheelType',
              'Nationality', 'Size', 'TopThreeAmericanName', 'VNST']
    df[feature_obj] = df[feature_obj].astype('category')

    ### Deal with Missings
    
    # 1. Missings in WheelType as own category:
    df['WheelType'] = df['WheelType'].cat.add_categories('unknown').fillna('unknown')

    # 2. Replace missings in numerical features with median

        #before: set 0 and 1 Dollar as missings
    null_cols = ['MMRAcquisitionAuctionAveragePrice',
                'MMRAcquisitionAuctionCleanPrice', 'MMRAcquisitionRetailAveragePrice',
                'MMRAcquisitonRetailCleanPrice', 'MMRCurrentAuctionAveragePrice',
                'MMRCurrentAuctionCleanPrice', 'MMRCurrentRetailAveragePrice',
                'MMRCurrentRetailCleanPrice', 'VehBCost']
    
    import numpy as np
    df[null_cols] = df[null_cols].replace([0, 1], np.nan)
    
        # Replace missings of nummerical features with median (calculated from train data)
    df = df.fillna(median)
    
    # 3. Categorical features: replace missings with modus (calculated from train data)
    cat_cols = df.select_dtypes(include=['category']).columns
    df[cat_cols] = df[cat_cols].fillna(mode)

    # Trim Outliers (winsorizing) in VehBCost using median and mad from train data
    if outlier_col is not None:
        lower = median[outlier_col] - 3 * mad[outlier_col]
        upper = median[outlier_col] + 3 * mad[outlier_col]
        df[outlier_col] = df[outlier_col].clip(lower=lower, upper=upper)
    
    return df, median, mode, mad

def trim_outliers_new(df, col, median=None, mad=None):
    '''
    Trims outliers of new features after (!) engineer function on train and test data
    
    col: List of columns
    
    procedure as in clean_data: fit only with info from train, transform train and test

        features_test                              = trim_outliers_new(features_test, ['col1', 'col2'], 
                                                                        median_new, mad_new)
    '''
    
    if median is None:
        median = df[col].median()
        
    from scipy.stats import median_abs_deviation
    
    if mad is None:
        mad = df[col].apply(median_abs_deviation)
    
    for c in col:
        lower = median[c] - 3 * mad[c]
        upper = median[c] + 3 * mad[c]
        df[c] = df[c].clip(lower=lower, upper=upper)
    
    return df, median, mad

Function for performing PCA on all MMR features

def pca_data (df, pca_pipeline=None):
    '''
    Performs PCA on MMR Variables to reduce to one dimension keeping 90% of the variance
    Perform after feature_engineering, before trim_outliers_new (use pca_cols as input)
    Application:
    features_train, pca_pipeline = pca_data(features_train)
    features_test                = pca_data(features_test, pca_pipeline)
    '''

    df = df.copy()
    
    if pca_pipeline is None:
        pca_pipeline = Pipeline([
            ('scale', StandardScaler()),
            ('pca',   PCA(n_components=0.9, svd_solver='full'))
        ])
        pca_pipeline.fit(df[features_mmr])  #fit
    
    # Transform
    pca_result = pca_pipeline.transform(df[features_mmr])
    n_components = pca_pipeline.named_steps['pca'].n_components_
    pca_cols = [f'pca_{i}' for i in range(n_components)]
    
    # Add component as new column, remove original MMR features
    df[pca_cols] = pca_result
    df = df.drop(columns=features_mmr)
    
    return df, pca_pipeline, pca_cols

def engineer_features(df, top_categories=None, col_n=None):
    '''
    recoding features and adding new ones
        
    # Make: first four categories + all others as fifth = others
    # Size: first five categories + all others as sixth = others
    # Color: first five categories + all others as sixth = others

    # Recode using .apply und lambda
    # logical meaning:
    
    if x in Colortop5:
    return x        # keep value
    else:
        return 'other'  # replace value with 'other'

    Application: features_train, top_categories = engineer_features(features_train)
                features_test                  = engineer_features(features_test, top_categories)
    '''
    
    #recode features
    
    df = df.copy()
    
    if col_n is None:
        col_n = {
            'Make': 4,
            'Size':  5,
            'Color':  5
        }
    
    if top_categories is None:
        top_categories = {}
        for col, n in col_n.items():
            top_categories[col] = df[col].value_counts().nlargest(n).index.tolist()
    
    for col in col_n.keys():
        df[col] = df[col].apply(lambda x: x if x in top_categories[col] else 'other')
    
    # create new feature
    df['Warr/Cost'] = df['WarrantyCost'] / df['VehBCost']
    
    return df, top_categories   
def pca_data (df, pca_pipeline=None):
    '''
    Performs PCA on MMR Variables to reduce to one dimension keeping 90% of the variance
    Perform after feature_engineering, before trim_outliers_new (use pca_cols as input)
    Application:
    features_train, pca_pipeline = pca_data(features_train)
    features_test                = pca_data(features_test, pca_pipeline)
    '''

    df = df.copy()
    
    if pca_pipeline is None:
        pca_pipeline = Pipeline([
            ('scale', StandardScaler()),
            ('pca',   PCA(n_components=0.9, svd_solver='full'))
        ])
        pca_pipeline.fit(df[features_mmr])  #fit
    
    # Transform
    pca_result = pca_pipeline.transform(df[features_mmr])
    n_components = pca_pipeline.named_steps['pca'].n_components_
    pca_cols = [f'pca_{i}' for i in range(n_components)]
    
    # Add component as new column, remove original MMR features
    df[pca_cols] = pca_result
    df = df.drop(columns=features_mmr)
    
    return df, pca_pipeline, pca_cols

def trim_outliers_new(df, col, median=None, mad=None):
    '''
    Trims outliers of new features after (!) engineer function on train and test data
    
    col: List of columns
    
    procedure as in clean_data: fit only with info from train, transform train and test
    
    Application: features_train, median_new, mad_new = trim_outliers_new(features_train, ['col1', 'col2'])
        features_test                              = trim_outliers_new(features_test, ['col1', 'col2'], 
                                                                        median_new, mad_new)
    '''

    df = df.copy()
    
    if median is None:
        median = df[col].median()
        
    from scipy.stats import median_abs_deviation
    
    if mad is None:
        mad = df[col].apply(median_abs_deviation)
    
    for c in col:
        lower = median[c] - 3 * mad[c]
        upper = median[c] + 3 * mad[c]
        df[c] = df[c].clip(lower=lower, upper=upper)
    
    return df, median, mad
def transform_features(df, cat_cols, num_cols, ct=None):
    '''
    Transforms features using OneHotEncoder for categorical and StandardScaler for numerical columns.
    
    cat_cols and num_cols have to be defined beforehand
    
    Application: features_train_t, ct = transform_features(features_train, cat_cols, num_cols)
               features_test_t, _  = transform_features(features_test, cat_cols, num_cols, ct)
    '''
    
    if ct is None:
        ct = ColumnTransformer(transformers=[
            ('onehot', OneHotEncoder(handle_unknown='ignore', sparse_output=False), cat_cols),
            ('scale',  StandardScaler(), num_cols)
        ], remainder='drop')
        df_transformed = ct.fit_transform(df)
    else:
        df_transformed = ct.transform(df)
    
    # resemble column names
    ohe_cols = ct.named_transformers_['onehot'].get_feature_names_out(cat_cols)
    all_cols = list(ohe_cols) + list(num_cols)
    
    # create data frame
    df_transformed = pd.DataFrame(df_transformed, columns=all_cols)
    
    return df_transformed, ct

def final_pipe (csv, model):
    '''Predicts Bad-Buy-Cars on Aim Dataset
    Arguments: 
    csv (str): path to data file in csv format
    model (model): the trained model
    Returns: 1 = badbuy, 0 = not a badbuy
    '''
    #1. Read Data
    import pandas as pd
    df_aim = pd.read_csv(csv)

    #2. Load parameter
    params = joblib.load('all_params.joblib')

    #3. Run functions with loaded parameters
    df_aim, _ , _ , _  = clean_data(df_aim, params['median'], params['mode'], params['mad'], outlier_col='VehBCost')
    df_aim, _     = engineer_features(df_aim, params['top_categories'])
    
    #4. Define features for PCA and run PCA
    features_mmr = ['MMRAcquisitionAuctionAveragePrice','MMRAcquisitionAuctionCleanPrice', 
                'MMRAcquisitionRetailAveragePrice',
                'MMRAcquisitonRetailCleanPrice', 'MMRCurrentAuctionAveragePrice',
                'MMRCurrentAuctionCleanPrice', 'MMRCurrentRetailAveragePrice',
                'MMRCurrentRetailCleanPrice']
    
    df_aim, _ , _     = pca_data(df_aim, params['pca_pipeline'])
    
    #5. Define features that need additional outlier trimming 
    outlier_cols = ['Warr/Cost'] + pca_cols
    
    df_aim, _ , _  = trim_outliers_new(df_aim, outlier_cols, params['median_new'], params['mad_new'])
    
    #6. Define features for onehot encoding and standardization and transform those features
    cat_cols = ['Auction', 'Make', 'Color', 'WheelType', 'Nationality', 'Size', 'TopThreeAmericanName']
    num_cols = ['VehYear', 'VehicleAge', 'VehOdo', 'VehBCost', 'WarrantyCost', 'Warr/Cost', 'pca_0']
    
    df_aim, _     = transform_features(df_aim, cat_cols, num_cols, params['ct'])

    #7. Predict
    predictions = model.predict(df_aim)
    return predictions
